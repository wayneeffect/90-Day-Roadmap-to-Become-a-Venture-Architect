**A realistic 90-day roadmap** focuses on building the core skills, portfolio evidence, network, and positioning that Venture Architects need—strategy, business modeling, market validation, financial analysis, entrepreneurial execution, and stakeholder communication. Experience (consulting, startups, product, or innovation roles) usually matters more than credentials alone, so treat this as intensive preparation to make yourself competitive for entry, junior, or transition opportunities.

This assumes you already have some professional experience (e.g., business, consulting, product, engineering, or related fields). Adjust intensity based on your starting point. Track progress weekly and treat it like a full-time project.

### Days 1–30: Foundations, Self-Assessment & Core Knowledge
**Goal**: Understand the role deeply, close knowledge gaps, and map your transferable experience.

- **Week 1 – Assess and orient**:
  - Audit your background: List projects involving strategy, market research, business models, product launches, financial analysis, or cross-functional leadership. Reframe them in Venture Architect language (e.g., “designed and validated a new revenue stream” rather than “worked on a project”).
  - Research 10–15 recent Venture Architect job postings (BCG X / Digital Ventures, venture studios, corporate innovation teams). Note required skills and language.
  - Read key materials: *The Lean Startup* (Eric Ries), *Business Model Generation* / Lean Canvas (Osterwalder/Maurya), and short guides on corporate venturing or venture building.

- **Weeks 2–4 – Master frameworks**:
  - Learn and practice Lean Canvas and Business Model Canvas (complete 3–5 for real or hypothetical ideas).
  - Study basic unit economics, market sizing (TAM/SAM/SOM), competitive analysis, and customer discovery techniques.
  - Build foundational financial modeling skills in Excel/Google Sheets: simple revenue forecasts, cost structures, runway, and basic P&L for early-stage ventures. Use free startup financial model templates.
  - Daily/weekly habit: Analyze one real startup or corporate venture case (what worked, assumptions tested, pivots).

**Milestone**: A personal “gap analysis” document + completed canvases and a basic financial model.

### Days 31–60: Hands-On Practice & Portfolio Building
**Goal**: Create tangible evidence of Venture Architect capabilities through real or simulated work.

- Design and validate 1–2 original venture concepts (or improve existing ones):
  - Conduct customer discovery (10–20 interviews via networks, LinkedIn, or communities).
  - Build full Lean Canvas + supporting research (market, competitors, trends).
  - Create a go-to-market outline and simple financial model with unit economics and scenarios.
- Run a mini “venture sprint”: Time-box ideation → validation → iteration (1–2 weeks per cycle). Document assumptions, tests, and learnings.
- Deepen complementary skills: Practice stakeholder-style presentations, competitive landscaping, and basic product/tech feasibility thinking.
- Optional accelerators: Complete free/affordable courses or modules on strategy consulting skills, lean startup, or financial modeling for startups (Coursera, YouTube, or firm resources). If relevant, explore an MBA-related certificate or short program, but prioritize practical output.
- Side activity: Volunteer for or contribute to a startup, innovation project, or internal corporate venture idea to gain live experience.

**Milestone**: 1–2 polished case studies (problem → research → model → recommendations → learnings) ready for a portfolio. Aim for one that demonstrates end-to-end thinking from idea to commercial viability.

### Days 61–90: Network, Position & Launch Applications
**Goal**: Make yourself visible, refine materials, and start converting preparation into opportunities.

- **Build visibility and network** (highest-ROI activity):
  - Optimize LinkedIn: Headline and about section focused on venture building/strategy/innovation. Share 4–6 posts or short analyses of markets, business models, or case studies.
  - Conduct 15–25 informational conversations: Reach out to current/former Venture Architects, people at BCG X, venture studios, corporate innovation teams, or startup founders. Ask about day-to-day work, hiring signals, and advice. Aim for warm introductions.
  - Join relevant communities (venture studio networks, startup groups, innovation forums) and contribute thoughtfully.

- **Prepare application materials**:
  - Resume: Lead with quantified impact and venture-relevant language. Include a short “Selected Venture Projects” section featuring your portfolio work.
  - Portfolio/one-pager: 2–4 case studies showing business modeling, validation, and strategic thinking.
  - Practice interviews: Prepare stories for behavioral questions (ambiguity, stakeholder management, zero-to-one execution) and case-style problem-solving (market entry, business model design).

- **Apply and iterate**:
  - Target 8–15 roles (entry/associate-level Venture Architect, innovation/strategy roles in studios or corporates, or related positions that serve as bridges).
  - Follow up strategically and request feedback.
  - Continue one ongoing side project or learning loop so you stay sharp.

**Milestone by Day 90**: Strong portfolio, active network of 15–30 relevant contacts, tailored applications submitted, and interview readiness. You should be able to clearly articulate how you approach opportunity scanning, design, validation, and commercialization.

### Ongoing Habits & Success Tips
- Dedicate consistent time (e.g., 15–25 hours/week if working full-time).
- Emphasize an entrepreneurial “builder” mindset: bias to action, comfort with ambiguity, and ownership.
- Track metrics: Number of canvases/models completed, interviews conducted, portfolio pieces finished, conversations held, applications sent.
- Realistic expectation: 90 days can make you a strong candidate and generate interviews, especially if you already have relevant experience. Landing the exact role may take longer depending on your background, location, and market conditions. Bridge roles (strategy, product, innovation, junior consulting) often accelerate the path.

This roadmap prioritizes demonstrable skills and evidence over pure credentials. Consistency in building real artifacts and relationships will differentiate you. Adjust based on your current strengths—lean harder into gaps (e.g., more modeling if quantitative skills are weaker). Start today with the self-assessment and one Lean Canvas.
